Every month now has a beginning and an end, and both of them are yours. You start the month by planning it, you look back over it and judge what you actually spent, and closing it hands you a report that says how the plan and the reality really compared. A month is no longer something that quietly passes by — it is something you plan, review and settle.

- Starting a month is a single, deliberate screen: set the allowance, see the fixed expenses and subscriptions already committed, plan an amount for every budget, and say how much you mean to draw from your savings reserve
- The plan is checked against what the month can actually afford, so you can never plan away money that is not there — and a draw from the reserve can never be more than the reserve holds
- Coconut never invents a month: the app waits for you, and a month only ever comes into existence because you started it
- Change your mind as often as you like — every adjustment to a budget or to the draw is recorded as a decision, so later you can see exactly how the plan was arrived at
- Closing a month is a proper review: fixed expenses, subscriptions and every budget side by side, ending in a confirmation that says plainly that closing is final and cannot be undone
- "How useful was your spending?" — look back over the month and mark each purchase a regret, neutral or good. Anything you never got around to counts as neutral, so a month can always be closed
- Closing opens near the end of the month, and if that window passes Coconut settles the month for you — so a month is never left hanging open
- The month-end report is fuller: every budget now shows what it started at and what you adjusted it to before closing, so a re-planned budget finally explains itself
- The reserve draw is part of the report too — the draw you planned with and the draw you finished the month with, kept clearly apart from the money that actually moved at month end
